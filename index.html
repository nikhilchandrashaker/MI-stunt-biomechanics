Method and limits

  • Falls and wall slams. Peak deceleration is height divided by stopping distance. The chance of death rises on a logistic curve around 140 g, which matches a fall of about 14 m onto a hard surface.
  • Vehicle strikes. A logistic curve in impact speed, centered near 41 mph, shaped like published pedestrian-crash data.
  • Explosions. Overpressure from the Kinney-Graham scaling formula for TNT-equivalent charges, then a logistic curve in log pressure centered near 240 kPa.
  • Breath-hold. A logistic curve in seconds underwater, centered at 120 s, with half of blackouts assumed fatal.
  • Fights. A flat 0.12% chance per blow of a lethal head injury.
  • Accumulation. Hazard of the later events in a film is multiplied by 1 + alpha times the number of earlier hits.

The curves are stylized fits from widely quoted figures, not fitted to fresh data, and I have not checked references. Event counts and effective speeds come from my memory of the films and are unverified. The totals show the order of magnitude, not a prediction.
